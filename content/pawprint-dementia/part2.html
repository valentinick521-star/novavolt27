As animals age, NAD+ metabolism changes. Mitochondrial function and cellular energy biology have consequently become major areas of aging research.

That does not mean declining NAD+ alone causes canine dementia.

And it does not mean increasing NAD+ has been proven to reverse it.

But it does raise an important question:

If the aging brain depends heavily on cellular energy…should a cognitive-support strategy look only at the brain—or also at the energy machinery supporting it?

That question creates an entirely different category of supplement.

Why “Dog Dementia Supplement” Can Mean Three Completely Different Things

Walk through the search results and almost everything gets placed under the same label:

Brain supplement.

But underneath that label are very different approaches.

Three different strategies

Not every cognitive supplement is trying to solve the same nutritional problem.

1
Approach #1: Alternative Brain Fuel

Some diets and formulas emphasize MCTs and related nutritional strategies designed around providing alternative sources of energy to the aging brain.

There is meaningful dog-specific research behind this general nutritional category.

2
Approach #2: Traditional Cognitive Nutrients

Other formulas use ingredients such as:

  • SAMe
  • Phosphatidylserine
  • Antioxidants
  • Omega-3 fatty acids
  • Vitamins
  • Various botanical compounds

The theory here is usually direct nutritional support for processes associated with brain health.

3
Approach #3: Cellular-Energy Support

This is the newer category.

Instead of asking only:

"What nutrients can we give the brain?"

It asks:

"What about the cellular-energy machinery the brain depends on in the first place?"

That is where NAD+, NMN, mitochondrial biology and compounds such as CoQ10 begin entering the conversation.
